abstract: 'The commercialization of high-energy, high-voltage lithium-ion and next-generation batteries hinges on how precisely the cathode–electrolyte interphase (CEI) can be understood and controlled. Far from being a static boundary, the CEI is a dynamic, compositionally heterogeneous reaction zone in which oxidative decomposition products of the electrolyte accumulate, rearrange, and continuously evolve with cycling. This review (i) establishes the fundamentals of CEI formation, structure, and growth kinetics; (ii) surveys interphase behavior across cathode families—Ni-rich NCM/NMC, high-voltage spinels, Li-rich oxides, sodium/zinc/dual-ion systems, and solid-state cells; (iii) organizes stabilization strategies spanning electrolyte additives, solvation engineering, surface coating and doping, artificial CEIs, and dry-electrode processing; and (iv) compares advanced characterization tools including cryo-EM, in situ TEM, XPS/ToF-SIMS, and EIS/DRT. Crucially, the review then examines how artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) are reshaping the CEI research paradigm—through machine-learning interatomic potentials, physics-informed neural networks, high-throughput electrolyte and additive screening, the coupling of multiscale imaging with computational modeling, and data-driven battery management that diagnoses interfacial degradation. We close by articulating an integratedexperiment–simulation–AI vision for the predictive design of the CEI.'
