abstract: 'The dry-electrode (solvent-free) process has moved from a laboratory concept to an industrial reality and is now the manufacturing paradigm most likely to displace conventional slurry casting in lithium-ion and next-generation batteries. By forming electrodes through a powder-to-film route rather than a wet slurry, it eliminates the toxic N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) solvent, the long, energy-intensive drying ovens, and the drying-induced binder migration, while enabling ultra-thick, high-loading electrodes. This review first organizes the fundamentals of dry processing—the powder-to-film concept, polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) fibrillation, and solvent-free bonding and microstructure formation. It then surveys the principal manufacturing routes (binder fibrillation with hot-pressing/calendering, electrostatic and dry spray, cold-plasma and melt processing, spray-drying, and roll-to-roll scale-up), the binder chemistries that govern adhesion and interfacial stability—PTFE and its electrochemical limitations, protective co-binders and coatings, fluorine-free and PFAS-compliant binders, and conductive binders—and the conductive-agent and mixing science that controls tortuosity and enables high-loading and all-solid-state architectures. Crucially, the review then examines how artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping dry-electrode manufacturing across its entire chain: machine-learning screening of materials and binders, data-driven optimization of mixing, fibrillation, and calendering, digital twins and surrogate models, computer-vision microstructure quantification and in-line defect detection, physics-informed models of degradation and safety, and AI-based cell diagnostics. Finally, it addresses the industrial implementation requirements—throughput, yield, in-line quality control, cost and energy savings, PFAS compliance, and the present commercial landscape (Tesla/Maxwell, LG Energy Solution, PowerCo, and others)—and closes with an integrated materials–process–AI–industry vision for the predictive, self-optimizing dry-electrode fab.'
